# Stage 3: Interview barriers and existing solutions for visually impaired (VI) candidates

Research date: 2026-09-21. Some sources returned HTTP 403 (JobAccess employer pages, Taylor & Francis full text), so a few findings rely on search-result summaries of those pages. Where that happened it is flagged.

## Q1. Evidence on hiring discrimination against disabled/blind candidates, and on when to disclose

### Takeaway
Field experiments and employer surveys consistently show bias. Disabled applicants get about 26% fewer callbacks (Ameri et al.), blind job seekers receive the lowest employability ratings of any disability type tested, and employers hold strong *implicit* negative attitudes about blind people's competence. Disclosing an accommodation need during the interview, early or late, lowered hiring ratings in a controlled experiment. The research does not support either "disclose early" or "disclose late" as a safe choice.

### Cited Findings
- Ameri, Schur, Adya, Bentley, McKay & Kruse field experiment: 6,016 applications to accounting jobs, one-third disclosing spinal cord injury, one-third Asperger's, one-third no disability. Applicants with disabilities "received 26% fewer expressions of employer interest". The gap was concentrated among experienced applicants and was larger at small private firms (<15 employees), which are often outside ADA coverage — [NBER w21560](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21560) (NBER working paper; the peer-reviewed version appeared in ILR Review 2018).
- Scoping review of employer attitudes toward people with VI (17 articles since 2018, 9 from the USA) — [PMC11611842 / Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11611842/):
  - McDonnall & Antonelli (2018): employers held "strong negative implicit attitudes about the competence" of VI people, while explicit attitudes were around neutral (McDonnall & Crudden 2018, EABES mean 34.03). This implicit/explicit gap matters because interviewers may consciously believe they are fair.
  - 82.3% of 379 hiring managers did not know how VI employees perform tasks such as accessing materials or using email (McDonnall & Crudden 2018).
  - Berre (2023): 1,341 employers rated 11,939 vignettes. Blind job seekers got the lowest employability rating (mean 1.6) of all disability types.
  - Fyhn et al. (2021, Norway): 17% rated a VI vignette character positively, against 70% for auditory impairment. Only 8% had previously had a VI colleague.
  - Papakonstantinou & Papadopoulos (2020, Greece): 81% of employers worried about communication with colleagues, and only 36.6% expressed positive hiring intentions.
  - Previous experience hiring someone with VI was "the greatest significant predictor" of positive attitudes. A single hour-long meeting with a vocational rehabilitation professional improved explicit attitudes and reduced implicit bias (IAT from 0.80 to 0.68; McDonnall & Antonelli 2022).
- RNIB survey: 656 blind and partially sighted respondents named employer attitudes as the biggest barrier to employment — [search summary of RNIB/Reed interview](https://www.reed.com/articles/creating-inclusive-workplaces-interview-with-the-RNIB).
- Disclosure timing, Ameri & Kurtzberg: 1,917 adults with management experience rated simulated interview clips. The applicant disclosed an accommodation need at the start, at the end, or not at all. Both early and late disclosure produced *poorer* employability ratings than no disclosure. Audio-only disclosure did worse than video disclosure — [Springer, J Cancer Survivorship 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11764-021-01142-3); [PubMed 35107798](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35107798/); popular write-up: [HBR, June 2024](https://hbr.org/2024/06/how-to-improve-the-hiring-process-for-disabled-candidates). Caveat: the condition studied was chronic pain, an invisible disability, not VI.
- Qualitative study on how disability is actually discussed in job interviews — [Disability & Society, 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09687599.2022.2162860) (full text returned 403, contents not verified).

### Inferences
- A blindness that the candidate cannot hide at an in-person interview produces the "surprise" scenario. The implicit-bias findings suggest surprise plus lack of knowledge ("how would they use email?") is the most damaging combination. The evidence that an hour of information shifts attitudes suggests a short "how I work" briefing sent before the interview could help. This is an inference and has not been tested for VI specifically.
- The Ameri & Kurtzberg result means a tool should not push disclosure. It should let the candidate choose to share *needs/adjustments* rather than a diagnosis.

### Gaps
- I found no study that directly compares interview outcomes for blind candidates who disclosed before the interview with those who disclosed at the door.
- No Vietnam-specific field experiment or callback study was found.

## Q2. Existing accommodation passports, disclosure tools and government schemes

### Takeaway
Mature schemes exist in the UK and Australia (Access to Work, EAF/JobAccess, Disability Confident guaranteed interviews, adjustment passports). They are mostly designed for employees already in post or for funding. Guaranteed-interview schemes require the candidate to declare a disability and are not enforceable. Large tech firms run central accommodation teams that small companies lack. There is no widely used, candidate-owned, pre-interview "needs card" that works for small employers.

### Cited Findings
- **Adjustment passports (UK):** Business Disability Forum describes a "Tailored Adjustments Plan", which some organisations call a passport or agreement. It is a living record of adjustments agreed between an employee and their line manager, and BDF offers a free template — [BDF](https://businessdisabilityforum.org.uk/resource/tailored-adjustments-plans-passports-and-agreements/). BDF also warns about the limitations: employers should not discuss disability during recruitment except in limited circumstances, and applicants have no legal obligation to disclose — [BDF: passports, law and limitations](https://businessdisabilityforum.org.uk/resource/adjustments-passports-the-law-their-use-and-their-limitations/). Result: passports are aimed at people already employed, not at candidates before an interview.
- **Australia Workplace Adjustment Passport:** JobAccess publishes a Workplace Adjustment Passport template (PDF dated 2025-07) — [JobAccess PDF](https://jobaccess.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2025-07/8136-workplace-adjustment_2.pdf).
- **Australia Employment Assistance Fund (EAF):** interview adjustments count as a "reasonable adjustment". EAF can fund an Auslan interpreter for job interviews, with the claim due up to 5 working days after the interview. Eligibility checks can take up to 10 working days — [JobAccess EAF](https://www.jobaccess.gov.au/i-am-a-person-with-disability/looking-applying-job/government-services-help-you/funding-workplace-changes/applying-eaf); [JobAccess interview support page (search summary; page returned 403)](https://www.jobaccess.gov.au/people-with-disability/support-you-may-need-for-an-interview). No VI-specific interview funding line was confirmed.
- **UK Access to Work, job interview strand:** funds a communication support worker or BSL interpreter at a job interview. Eligibility: an interview date for a paid job or apprenticeship, age 16+, and living in England, Scotland or Wales — [GOV.UK Easy Read](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1125481/access-to-work-communication-support-at-a-job-interview.pdf). The interview strand covers *communication* support, so it has little relevance to blind candidates, whose main needs are access to materials and orientation. A third-party guide reports average processing of around 109 days — [finallyseen.org.uk (not official, unverified)](https://finallyseen.org.uk/guides/access-to-work-grant).
- **Disability Confident "offer an interview" (guaranteed interview) scheme:** disabled applicants who declare a disability and meet the minimum criteria are offered an interview. It is **not legally binding**, employers may make exceptions in high-volume recruitment, and only Level 3 involves external validation. The government announced reforms in January 2026 — [Stammeringlaw](https://www.stammeringlaw.org.uk/employment/recruitment-promotion/disability-confident-guaranteed-interviews/). About 19,000 employers are signed up, the disability employment gap is close to 30 percentage points, and a Reform Delivery Plan runs from Dec 2025 to Dec 2026 — [PSR summary](https://publicsectorresourcing.co.uk/disability-confident-scheme/); [GOV.UK Easy Read delivery plan](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/698ca01d2f683cc788c287b3/disability-confident-reform-delivery-plan-december-2025-to-december-2026-easy-read.pdf).
- **Microsoft:** candidates can request accommodations at any time without disclosing specifics of their disability, only their needs. Requests for a specific screen or interview wait until the candidate is invited. Offered accommodations include sign language interpreters, CART captioning and "Accessibility Partners for the blind". The accommodations team is centralised and trained in confidentiality — [Microsoft Careers Accessibility](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/accessibility.html); [Microsoft Accessible Hiring](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/hiring).
- **Mentra** (neurodiversity only): one application, and AI evaluates "over 76 data points" including accommodations and sensitivities in a "reverse job fair". The company reports 97% retention and 79% of recommended candidates being selected for interview (self-reported) — [Microsoft Accessibility Blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/accessibility/mentra-is-reimagining-neurodiverse-employment/); [Mentra](https://www.mentra.com/workplace-accommodations).
- **Job Accommodation Network (JAN) cost data:** of 1,425 employers who gave cost information, 61% said the accommodation cost nothing, 33% reported a one-time cost with a **median of $300**, and 6% reported ongoing costs. The survey covered employers who contacted JAN in 2019–2024 (26,028 surveyed, 5,406 responses) — [JAN Costs and Benefits](https://askjan.org/topics/costs.cfm). A DOL 2023 release says nearly half of accommodations cost nothing — [US DOL](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/odep/odep20230504).

### Inferences
- The gap is a lightweight, candidate-owned "interview needs card" that: (a) states needs, not a diagnosis; (b) is sent after an interview invite, following the Microsoft model; (c) comes with a ready-made checklist for a small company that has no HR accommodation team. Nothing like this was found for SMEs or in Vietnam.
- JAN's "$300 median, 61% zero cost" figure is useful for countering employer cost fears. It is US data and should be labelled as such.

### Gaps
- Google's and EY's candidate accommodation processes were not verified (no search budget left). Inclusively's model was not verified.
- I found no ATS vendor (Workday, Greenhouse, etc.) with a documented native "candidate adjustment request" feature. Not confirmed either way.

## Q3. Interviewer training and etiquette guidance for blind candidates

### Takeaway
Guidance from AFB, Blind Citizens Australia, RNIB and APH agrees on the basics: ask before guiding, describe the room, give clear arrival directions, provide materials in accessible formats, allow extra time for tests, don't pet guide dogs, and ask only job-related questions. This guidance sits in long PDFs and web pages and is not delivered to the interviewer at the moment it is needed.

### Cited Findings
- On greeting, ask whether the person wants assistance; some take your arm, others prefer verbal directions. Describe the room ("round table, your chair is on your left"). How or when someone lost their sight is not job-relevant. Never pet a guide dog. Candidates should state needed adjustments, including accessible document formats, after accepting an interview — [AFB HR toolkit: Application, Assessment, Interviewing](https://www.afb.org/research-and-initiatives/research/toolkits/steps-success-hr-manager-inclusion/application); [Blind Citizens Australia recruiter guidance](https://eyetothefuture.com.au/hello-recruiters-how-to-successfully-recruit-a-person-who-is-blind-or-vision-impaired/) (points merged from a search summary of both sources).
- RNIB: the recruitment process, including application, shortlisting and interview, is covered by non-discrimination law. Adjustments can include accessible or large-print application forms and extra time for interview tests. Employers should tell applicants how to arrive (public transport, which door to use) and brief reception — [RNIB employer guide PDF](https://www.rnib.org.uk/documents/303/Employing_someone_with_sight_loss_guide.pdf).
- Candidate-side guidance on showing capability in an interview — [APH ConnectCenter](https://aphconnectcenter.org/careerconnect/job-seekers/interview-preparation/demonstrating-capability/); [AFB AccessWorld interview tips](https://afb.org/aw/12/10/15986).

### Inferences
- A hackathon-feasible idea: a one-page, auto-generated "interviewer brief" produced from the candidate's needs card. It would cover etiquette, room setup, material formats and questions to avoid, and would be sent to the interviewer and receptionist 24 hours before the interview. This turns long guidance into a just-in-time checklist.

### Gaps
- NFB-specific interviewer guidance and Vision Australia's own employer guidance were not fetched directly.
- No evidence was found on whether etiquette training changes interview outcomes, as distinct from attitudes (see the Q1 attitude-intervention finding).

## Q4. Online interview platforms (Zoom, Teams, Meet) and screen-reader accessibility

### Takeaway
Comparative studies find that no major platform is fully accessible by screen reader and keyboard. Zoom is preferred by screen-reader users. Screen sharing is a major blind spot because shared slides are invisible to screen readers. Candidates face a different platform for each employer.

### Cited Findings
- A study of the desktop and mobile versions of Zoom, Google Meet and MS Teams combined inspection evaluations with surveys of VI users (65 desktop and 94 mobile respondents). Zoom was preferred, but "none of the tools was fully accessible via screen reader and keyboard or gestures" — [ACM TACCESS, doi 10.1145/3573012](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3573012) (search summary).
- Reported problems include information presented in purely visual form, moving between areas, hosting, joining or accepting invitations, and controlling speakers and cameras when access keys are missing. Content shared through Zoom screen share is unreadable by screen readers — [T&F comparative study of Zoom/Teams/Meet/Skype, 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0144929X.2023.2286533) (full text returned 403, based on search summary).
- A survey of blind and low-vision users rated Zoom easiest, followed by Google Meet, citing "clear audio, easy navigation, labeled buttons, useful hotkeys" — [Knapp Strategic survey](https://www.knappstrategic.com/accessiversity-blog/survey-says-what-web-conferencing-solutions-are-best-for-blind-and-low-vision-users) (consultancy blog, lower rigour).
- AFB review of Google Meet with assistive technology — [AFB AccessWorld](https://afb.org/aw/22/3/17454). ASSETS 2025 paper on blind and low-vision workers' use of collaboration tools — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3663547.3746332).

### Inferences
- Useful ideas that are practical to build: (a) a per-platform "join and control cheat sheet" of hotkeys for JAWS, NVDA and VoiceOver, sent with the invite; (b) a rule in the interviewer brief to send any shared material as an accessible file beforehand instead of relying on screen share; (c) a candidate preference field for the platform they would like to use.

### Gaps
- Vendor accessibility docs (Zoom, Teams and Meet keyboard shortcut pages) were not fetched. The current 2026 state of each platform may have improved since the 2022–2023 studies.

## Q5. Physical venue orientation and indoor wayfinding

### Takeaway
Commercial tools exist: NaviLens codes, GoodMaps LiDAR-mapped venues, and Aira remote sighted agents. They depend on venue installation or a subscription and are unlikely to be available at a typical Vietnamese SME office. The cheapest option is well-written text directions from the entrance to the interview room, which RNIB already recommends.

### Cited Findings
- NaviLens is a high-contrast colour code that a free phone app reads from far away and at wide angles, then plays preprogrammed audio. GoodMaps is a free app that gives step-by-step guidance in venues mapped with LiDAR (airports, stations, retail). Aira is a subscription service connecting users to trained sighted agents. A study of more than 10,000 Aira calls over 3 months found an average length of 8–9 minutes, used mostly for reading and navigation — [Optometry Times](https://www.optometrytimes.com/view/the-white-cane-and-beyond-part-2).
- GoodMaps positions its indoor navigation around European Accessibility Act compliance — [GoodMaps Connect](https://connect.goodmaps.com/docs/ensuring-compliance/). US government overview of indoor navigation — [ACL](https://acl.gov/ada/improving-indoor-navigation).
- RNIB: include transport options and which door to use, and brief reception — [RNIB guide](https://www.rnib.org.uk/documents/303/Employing_someone_with_sight_loss_guide.pdf).
- Vietnam: interviews held on upper floors without elevators are cited as a barrier. Candidates are advised to ask about the venue in advance or request an online first round — [CareerLink.vn, Aug 2026](https://www.careerlink.vn/cam-nang-viec-lam/dinh-huong-nghe-nghiep/nguoi-khuyet-tat-tim-viec-lam).

### Inferences
- A feasible hackathon feature: the employer fills in a guided form ("route from gate/grab drop-off, which lift, floor, room, landmarks, who meets you"). The tool produces screen-reader-friendly text directions, and optionally a meeting-point arrangement in which a named person meets the candidate at the entrance. This needs no hardware. NaviLens or GoodMaps is a stretch option.

### Gaps
- NaviLens and GoodMaps deployments in Vietnam were not confirmed. None were found.

## Q6. Does anything similar exist in Vietnam?

### Takeaway
Vietnam has anti-discrimination law and tax incentives, and NGOs run job fairs and workshops. No structured interview-accommodation request tool, adjustment passport or interviewer-briefing product for VI candidates was found.

### Cited Findings
- About 7 million people with disabilities (7% of the population aged 2+). 31.7% of people with disabilities aged 15+ are employed, against 83.8% of those without. Employers worry about "adaptability" and workplace accessibility (raised lettering, signage) rather than qualifications. Organisations involved include Samaritan's Purse Vietnam, the Nguyễn Văn Tố vocational centre and the "Bàn tay khéo léo" centre — [VOV2, June 2024](https://vov2.vov.vn/doi-song-xa-hoi/tang-co-hoi-viec-lam-cho-sinh-vien-khiem-thi-48737.vov2).
- Legal basis: Law on Persons with Disabilities 51/2010 and Labour Code 45/2019 prohibit disability discrimination in hiring. Employers must make adjustments that do not impose an undue burden. Businesses with a workforce that is at least 30% disabled qualify for corporate income tax exemption and preferential loans. The article also notes that accessibility infrastructure is uneven — [CareerLink.vn, Aug 2026](https://www.careerlink.vn/cam-nang-viec-lam/dinh-huong-nghe-nghiep/nguoi-khuyet-tat-tim-viec-lam).
- Media reports say interviewers focus on health rather than expertise, and that many businesses are reluctant to hire people with disabilities — [CareerViet](https://careerviet.vn/vi/talentcommunity/nhieu-doanh-nghiep-ngai-tuyen-dung-nguoi-khuyet-tat.35A50ECE.html); [Phụ nữ Việt Nam, Apr 2025](https://phunuvietnam.vn/nguoi-khuyet-tat-kho-tim-viec-vi-dinh-kien-tu-gia-dinh-va-xa-hoi-20250418142642274.htm) (from a search summary; exact attribution per article not verified).

### Inferences
- The combination of mostly SME employers, no HR accommodation function and a legal duty to make adjustments means a Vietnamese-language, zero-cost "interview readiness kit" fills a real gap. It would cover the candidate needs card, the interviewer brief, text venue directions and a platform cheat sheet. The legal citations (51/2010, 45/2019) can be presented to employers as "you're already obliged to do this".

### Gaps
- The CareerLink claim about the 30% threshold for tax incentives comes from a job-portal article. It should be checked against Decree 28/2012 or the current implementing decree before being quoted.
- No Vietnamese data was found on interview-stage outcomes for VI candidates, or on which video platforms Vietnamese employers use for interviews.
