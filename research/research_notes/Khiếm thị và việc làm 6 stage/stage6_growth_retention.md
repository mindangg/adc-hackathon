# Stage 6: Growth and Retention of Visually Impaired (VI) Employees (skill development, career progression, leadership, retention)

## Q1. How accessible are online professional development platforms (Coursera, edX, LinkedIn Learning, Udemy) and MOOCs for screen reader users?

### Takeaway
Academic evaluations keep finding serious screen reader barriers in MOOCs: missing alt text, menus that are hard to reach, no way to skip repeated content, missing language markup, and quizzes that are hard to use. edX has usually scored best. Vendors say they aim for WCAG, but that is not the same as independent proof that the platforms work for blind learners. No evaluation from 2024–2026 was found.

### Cited Findings
- Park, So & Cha (2019, *Australasian Journal of Educational Technology* 35(6)): a user study with 3 VI university students plus a WCAG 2.0 heuristic walkthrough by 5 evaluators. It concluded that "serious accessibility issues exist in MOOC platforms" that stop VI learners from taking full part. Recommendations: alt text for non-text media, state information for hidden menu elements, bypass buttons for repeated content, downloadable lectures with subtitles, and Universal Design for Learning. — [Park et al., AJET 2019](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/download/5521/1636/18674)
- The same paper's literature review reports that a blind user tested Udacity, Coursera, edX, OpenCourseWorld and Iversity, and "only edX was accessible". It also reports a prior Coursera study (2 experts plus 3 blind screen reader users) that found barriers for VI learners. Another reviewed study ranked accessibility from high to low as edX, FutureLearn, UNED, then Coursera, MiriadaX, Udemy, Udacity. — [Park et al., AJET 2019](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/download/5521/1636/18674)
- A study of 5 MOOC platforms with blind users found that none was fully accessible and that edX was the only one where all tasks could be completed. The main problem across platforms was missing language markers for screen readers. — [The Accessibility of MOOCs for Blind Learners (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320542894_The_Accessibility_of_MOOCs_for_Blind_Learners)
- Coursera evaluations against WCAG 2.1 found frequent missing alt text for multimedia, drop-down menus that are hard to access, and no bypass buttons. — [Universal Access in the Information Society, Springer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-022-00927-2)
- MOOC providers' own view of accessibility is covered in [Iniesto et al., Journal of Interactive Media in Education](https://jime.open.ac.uk/articles/10.5334/jime.430).
- Udemy's accessibility statement says it "strives to follow" WCAG 2.1 and WAI-ARIA, publishes a VPAT, and tests with NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver and TalkBack. — [Udemy Accessibility Statement](https://about.udemy.com/accessibility-statement/)
- edX gives course teams guidance on accessible content (alt text, transcripts, accessible problem types). — [edX docs: Supporting Learners with Diverse Needs](https://edx.readthedocs.io/projects/edx-partner-course-staff/en/latest/accessibility/supporting_learners_diverse_needs.html)

### Inferences
- A platform that meets WCAG does not make every course accessible. Course content such as slides, charts, code screenshots, and instructor-uploaded PDFs or videos without description is often the real barrier, and each instructor controls that.
- Coursera and edX have Vietnamese learners, so they are relevant in Vietnam. Most content is in English, though, and the language-marker problem suggests extra friction for Vietnamese-language screen reader users.

### Gaps
- I did not verify current VPATs or accessibility statements for Coursera or LinkedIn Learning (search did not surface them). Check coursera.org and linkedin.com/accessibility directly.
- I found no independent MOOC accessibility study from 2024–2026, and no study of in-person corporate training accessibility for blind staff.
- I found no source quantifying the **cost of accessible training**, such as the cost to remediate a course, pay a reader or buy braille materials. Treat this as a gap. The only related data point: Crudden & Steverson respondents named "salary for a reader" as a service they needed (see Q3).

## Q2. What is the evidence on promotion gaps, pay gaps, leadership representation, and leadership bias?

### Takeaway
Pay and inclusion gaps are well documented. Direct promotion-rate data for blind workers is rare. The evidence points to managers seeing VI staff as suited to "specific jobs" rather than as promotable, to most disabled leaders not disclosing (so there are few visible role models), and to a large gap between what executives believe about inclusion and what disabled employees report.

### Cited Findings
- **ILO (2024):** globally, workers with disabilities earn 12% less per hour. About three-quarters of that gap (9 points) is not explained by education, age or type of work. The gap is 26% in low and lower-middle income countries and 9% in developed countries. — [ILO news on working paper](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/new-ilo-working-paper-exposes-significant-disability-wage-gap); [WEF summary](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2024/09/disabilities-workforce-inclusion-wage-gap-ilo/)
- **US blind and low vision workers:** blind or low vision workers with a bachelor's degree or higher earned about $14,727 less per year than non-disabled peers, and the gap is over $13,000 across all education levels. Degree holders with disabilities often work in lower-level jobs that do not match their qualifications. — [AFB, Disability Employment Research: Key Takeaways](https://afb.org/research-and-initiatives/employment/reviewing-disability-employment-research-people-blind-visually)
- **Manager bias:** in the NIB hiring-manager survey, nearly half said few jobs in their company suited blind or low vision workers, and many saw customer service as the most suitable role. AFB also names social and interpersonal communication challenges as a barrier to advancement. — [AFB](https://afb.org/research-and-initiatives/employment/reviewing-disability-employment-research-people-blind-visually). Mississippi State's summary says blind or VI workers "tend to be hired for a specific job and managers may not see them as potential candidates for promotion". — [search summary of MSU NRTC materials](https://www.blind.msstate.edu/sites/www.blind.msstate.edu/files/2020-04/Barriers_to_Employment_-_Executive_Summary.pdf) (not verified against the full text)
- **Accenture Getting to Equal 2020** (30,282 employees, including 5,870 with disabilities, plus 1,748 executives): — [Accenture/Disability:IN/AAPD 2020 PDF](https://www.odenetwork.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/EnablingChange-GettingToEqual2020Accenture.pdf)
  - Employees with disabilities are 1.6x (60%) more likely to feel excluded. Their sense of being included with real influence over decisions is 27% lower than peers'.
  - 76% of employees and 80% of leaders with disabilities are not fully open about their disability, which means fewer role models and mentors.
  - 71% of employees with disabilities rated "seeing people like me in senior leadership positions" as important for thriving (77% rated "freedom to be the same person at work as at home").
  - Perception gap: 67% of leadership think their technology set-ups and culture are supportive, but only 41% of employees with disabilities agree. Only 20% feel their organisation is fully committed to supporting them.
  - "More equal" cultures share 8 factors, including clear role models, ERGs, training, and fair and transparent pay.
- **Accenture "The disability inclusion imperative" (2023):** fear of "limited opportunities for growth" is a named reason for not disclosing. 80% of C-suite executives and their direct reports who have disabilities do not disclose. Only 4.6% of employees self-identify disability to employers (citing the 2023 DEI). Leading companies had 1.6x more revenue, 2.6x more net income and 2x more economic profit than other DEI participants. The share of "leaders" fell from 32% of 140 companies (2018) to 30% of 346 (2023). — [Accenture 2023 PDF](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-2/Disability-Inclusion-Report-Business-Imperative.pdf)
- **Disability:IN 2024 DEI:** the press release is titled "Strong growth in leadership and accessibility but boardroom inclusion lags". — [Business Wire 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240715463851/en/The-2024-Disability-Equality-Index-%C2%AE-Reveals-Strong-Growth-in-Leadership-and-Accessibility-but-Boardroom-Inclusion-Lags) (page blocked, 403; I could not extract the numbers)
- **Disability:IN 2025 Disability Index** (655 submissions, 8 countries): 90% of participants have a disability ERG, 99% offer flexible work, and 88% have an accommodations policy. Aggregate self-identification did not rise in 2025. — [Disability:IN 2025 report](https://disabilityin.org/resource/2025-disability-index-report/)
- **Leadership examples:** a blind CEO was appointed at ParalympicsGB in 2023, and Paul Arnold is Deputy CEO and COO of the UK Information Commissioner's Office. The same source says very few blind or low vision Americans reach top leadership. — [BlindAmbition: Leadership of Blind CEOs](https://www.blindambition.co.uk/blind-ceo-md-boss/); also [Chief Executive: Chad Foster](https://chiefexecutive.net/chad-foster-resilience-is-something-ceos-can-learn/)

### Inferences
- Senior roles filled through networks hurt VI staff twice: they are more often excluded socially (Accenture's 60% figure) and have fewer visible disabled leaders to sponsor them (76–80% non-disclosure).
- Network hiring evidence: a NY Fed staff report on informal referrals is widely cited as showing 30–50% of hires come through referrals. — [NY Fed SR568](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr568.pdf) (the figure comes from a search summary; check it in the paper). The common "85% of jobs are never posted" claim ([OpenArc](https://www.openarc.net/the-power-of-networking-why-85-of-jobs-are-never-posted-online-2025-data/)) has no strong primary source. **Do not use it.**

### Gaps
- I found no promotion-rate comparison specific to blind or VI employees, and no data on the share of blind people in management anywhere, including Vietnam.
- I found no reliable data on the share of *senior* roles filled by referral as opposed to all roles.
- I found no Vietnam-specific data on pay or promotion gaps for VI workers.

## Q3. What is known about retention and turnover of VI and disabled employees?

### Takeaway
The best VI-specific source (Crudden & Steverson 2021, n=388) shows two retention risks. First, people who lose vision mid-career usually lose that job (66.3%), mainly because the job was not modified or the employer was unwilling. Second, VI workers change employers often, mostly for better opportunities, which suggests they leave to advance.

### Cited Findings
All from Crudden, A. & Steverson, A.C. (2021), *Job retention and career advancement: A survey of persons who are blind or have low vision*, NRTC on Blindness and Low Vision, Mississippi State (n=388). — [PDF](https://www.blind.msstate.edu/sites/www.blind.msstate.edu/files/2021-07/Crudden%20&%20Steverson%20(2021)%20Job%20Retention%20and%20Career%20Advancement%20A%20Survey%20of%20Persons%20who%20are%20Blind%20or%20Have%20Low%20Vision.pdf)
- 72.2% had kept employment. Most were satisfied with their jobs **but were interested in career advancement**.
- 80.2% of 262 employed respondents had changed employers. Reasons: better opportunities elsewhere (59.5%), difficulty with a supervisor or management (29.1%), relocation (23.3%), inability to do the job because of vision (20.5%). 90.4% of those who changed had a positive outcome.
- 66.3% of those who lost vision while employed could not stay with that employer. Reasons: job could not be modified (37.8%), termination or employer unwillingness (31.1%), unaware of accommodations (20.0%), needed blindness-skills training (11.1%). Only 22.8% of those who told someone at work got help learning about accommodations.
- What helped people keep their job after vision loss: assistive technology (32.1%), employer cooperation (28.6%), blindness-skills training, coworker and peer support, and work environment changes (10.7% each).
- 33.1% believed they needed more training to be employed or advance. 46.2% of the 173 who had a mentor said the mentor helped them get a job. JAWS or another screen reader was the most helpful technology for keeping or advancing in a job (45.5%).
- Services respondents said they needed to keep or advance in a job: job development and search skills (27.3%), new assistive technology (18.2%), counselling (17.1%), technology training (14.8%), education or certification (11.4%). "Other" included salary for a reader.
- VR graduates who used technological aids had 91% employment, compared with 25% without them. — [AFB](https://afb.org/research-and-initiatives/employment/reviewing-disability-employment-research-people-blind-visually)
- Predictors of job satisfaction for VI workers (relationships with colleagues and supervisors, promotion opportunities; subsample of 195 from the 388): — [PMC10961960](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10961960/) (not read in full)

### Inferences
- There are two retention levers: (1) fast accommodation plus reskilling when an employee acquires vision loss, and (2) visible advancement paths so VI staff do not have to leave to move up.

### Gaps
- I found no turnover rate for disabled or VI employees compared with peers. The ILO and Disability:IN sources I reviewed did not give one.
- The sample is US-only and skewed (61% female, 80% white, mean age 50.5). It may not transfer to Vietnam.

## Q4. What mentoring, ERG, and career programs exist (the competitive landscape)?

### Takeaway
There are many programs, but they are mostly US or Australia-based, focused on getting a first job or on tech skills, and run by NGOs. Structured leadership development for blind professionals (AFB BLDP) is rare. Vietnam has strong skills training (Sao Mai) but little evidence of structured mentoring for mid-career advancement or leadership.

### Cited Findings
- **AFB Blind Leaders Development Program (US):** fellows develop leadership effectiveness and "soft/survival" skills, expand their networks, and learn from blind leader mentors. The first cohort was in 2020. — [AFB BLDP](https://afb.org/blind-leaders); [2020 inaugural class](https://afb.org/news-publications/press-room/press-release-archive/press-release-2020/inaugural-class-blind-leaders-development-program-fellows). AFB CareerConnect is a mentoring and careers information resource. — [AFB CareerConnect Mentor Guide](http://www.afb.org/info/living-with-vision-loss/for-job-seekers/make-connections/become-a-careerconnect-mentor/mentor-guide/12345)
- **Blind Institute of Technology (US):** a nonprofit staffing and recruiting agency for professionals with disabilities. Its BIT Academy (launched 2017) trains Salesforce Administration and Digital Accessibility Analysis. — [BIT](https://blindinstituteoftechnology.org/); [Zero Project](https://zeroproject.org/view/organization/341cf004-596b-ef11-bfe2-000d3ab254a3); [Sight Tech Global session](https://sighttechglobal.com/session/spotlight-bridging-the-gap-how-bit-opens-tech-careers-for-professionals-with-disabilities/)
- **Mentra (US):** an AI-driven employment network for **neurodivergent** job seekers (not VI), reported at 44,000+ candidates. It is a model for a disability-specific talent marketplace. — [Mentra](https://www.mentra.com/); [Microsoft Accessibility Blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/accessibility/mentra-is-reimagining-neurodiverse-employment/)
- **Vision Australia:** employment services for blind or low vision people, covering first job, "dream job", and strategies to succeed in a current job. It delivers the government's Inclusive Employment Australia program (which replaced DES from 1 Nov 2025) in Queensland, Victoria and New South Wales. — [Vision Australia Employment](https://www.visionaustralia.org/services/find-services/employment)
- **Corporate ERG and mentoring example:** Dell pairs new hires with a mentor from its True Ability ERG and runs an Assistive Technology Center of Excellence where employees can request AT privately. — [Accenture 2023](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-2/Disability-Inclusion-Report-Business-Imperative.pdf). 90% of Disability Index participants have a disability ERG. — [Disability:IN 2025](https://disabilityin.org/resource/2025-disability-index-report/)
- **Self-directed learning:**
  - **Sao Mai Center for the Blind (HCMC, Vietnam, founded 2001):** free training in assistive technology, vocational skills, soft skills, music and foreign languages. It also offers career guidance and job placement. It has set up 26 computer labs in 23 provinces and cities and trained 600+ AT trainers and 5,500+ VI people. — [Sao Mai About](https://saomaicenter.org/en/about); [Sao Mai Training](https://saomaicenter.org/en/training). It placed 32 people in jobs using NVDA and Office after training. — [NV Access: Closing the gap in Vietnam](https://www.nvaccess.org/post/closing_the_gap_in_vietnam/)
  - **Hadley (US):** free distance courses for adults with vision loss, including technology and business and employment skills, with one-to-one instructor support. — [Hadley](https://hadleyhelps.org/); [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/digital-learning/article/2017/03/29/hadley-institute-leads-digital-learning-blind)
- **Accenture's recommended actions:** mentoring and coaching, skilling and reskilling, accessible tools, and a formal accommodations program. — [Accenture 2018 report](https://www.accenture.com/_acnmedia/pdf-89/accenture-disability-inclusion-research-report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Gap in Vietnam: Sao Mai covers entry skills and placement well. Nothing found covers the post-hire stage: promotion readiness, sponsorship, accessible corporate training content, or leadership pipelines.

### Gaps
- I did not verify details of Microsoft's disability ERG or its Vietnam presence. I found no information on Perkins eLearning for adult workplace skills.
- I found no evaluation data (outcomes, promotion rates) for any mentoring program listed.
- I found no Vietnamese company disability ERGs.

## Q5. What are the gaps and hackathon-feasible ideas?

### Takeaway
The clearest gaps are post-hire: training content that is not accessible, promotion decisions made through informal networks, and no visible blind role models. All three can be addressed with lightweight software and matching tools that could be prototyped in a hackathon.

### Cited Findings (gap evidence recap)
- MOOC and course content barriers such as alt text, bypass, and language markup — [Park et al. 2019](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/download/5521/1636/18674)
- Mentors help (46.2% said a mentor helped them get a job), and 33.1% want more training — [Crudden & Steverson 2021](https://www.blind.msstate.edu/sites/www.blind.msstate.edu/files/2021-07/Crudden%20&%20Steverson%20(2021)%20Job%20Retention%20and%20Career%20Advancement%20A%20Survey%20of%20Persons%20who%20are%20Blind%20or%20Have%20Low%20Vision.pdf)
- 71% value seeing people like them in senior leadership, and 76–80% do not disclose — [Getting to Equal 2020](https://www.odenetwork.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/EnablingChange-GettingToEqual2020Accenture.pdf)
- Leaders overrate how supportive they are (67% of leaders say supportive, 41% of employees agree) — same source

### Inferences (hackathon ideas; my proposals, not sourced solutions)
1. **Accessible training converter or checker:** upload a course (slides, PDF, video) and get flagged problems (missing alt text, undescribed charts, missing language tags). An LLM drafts image and chart descriptions and outputs a screen-reader-ready Vietnamese and English version. This targets the course-content gap.
2. **Anonymous inclusion pulse:** a short, screen-reader-first survey comparing what managers perceive with what VI staff report, modelled on Accenture's 67% vs 41% gap. It gives HR a dashboard.
3. **Blind mentor and sponsor matching (Vietnam):** match VI professionals with blind or sighted senior sponsors, building on Sao Mai alumni and AFB BLDP-style cohorts. It works against network-only senior hiring.
4. **Internal opportunity board:** a voice-accessible feed of internal stretch projects and roles that are otherwise shared informally, with accessibility requirements listed up front.
5. **Promotion-readiness portfolio:** a screen-reader-friendly log of achievements and skills to counter "hired for one specific job" bias.
6. **Vision-loss retention playbook bot:** a step-by-step guide for employers when an employee acquires vision loss (accommodations, AT, referral to Sao Mai). It targets the 66.3% job loss rate.

### Gaps
- None of these ideas has been validated with Vietnamese VI employees. Suggest quick interviews through Sao Mai.
- The cost of accessible training is still unquantified (see Q1).
